(1)

In the autumn of 1983, the factory gave out a round of bonuses, and the men on Hongqi Street started pouring Sanbai into their mouths in the afternoon. It wasn't dark yet, but there were already three circles of people gathered under the locust tree at the entrance of the street. The smell of cigarettes, alcohol and freshly cooked cabbage and vermicelli mixed together, making the whole street look like a slow-boiling cauldron.

Hongqi Street is not an alley, but a section of dirt road outside the family area of ​​the factory office. There are gray brick bungalows on both sides. Each family has its own courtyard gate. There is usually only one main room and a kitchen in the courtyard gate. The man stands in front of the machine tool for eight hours during the day, and when he returns to this stretch of street less than 200 meters away at night, there are not many things he can do for fun: playing chess, bragging, listening to storytelling on the radio, and beating his wife.

This is not a curse, it is a statement. Whose daughter-in-law was slapped a few times and knelt down for half an hour, but when she was buying groceries the next day, someone still laughed and asked, "Did you clean up last night?" Being ruthless means that the man has a temper and respect; being ruthless means that he is a coward and cannot even control the people in the room. As for whether the daughter-in-law is in pain or not, and whether she is willing to do so, is not within the scope of discussion - that is a "family matter", and family matters, in Hongqi Street, are men's affairs.

Sun Guiying married into the street in autumn.

Her man's name is Zhao Tiezhu, the monitor of the machine repair class. He is 1.78 meters tall, with broad shoulders and calluses on his palms. On the day of the wedding, Aunt Wang at the street corner held an enamel jar and said to people: "Tiezhu's wife is so smart, she just doesn't know how to survive." Everyone laughed. Sun Guiying stood on the threshold of the new house, her face as hot as a newly uncovered cauldron. It was neither a smile nor a smile.

She comes from a rural area thirty miles away. Her father is an honest farmer and her mother has never spoken loudly in her life. Sun Guiying has known since she was a child that when a woman gets married, she just changes her life to another family; whether her life is good or not depends on whether the man is willing to give you food and love you on the kang - as for other things, she has never heard of it, nor has she thought about it.

On the third day after the wedding, Zhao Tiezhu came back after drinking half a catty of Baigan. He thought the porridge she served was too thin, so he threw the bowl on the ground. Sun Guiying knelt down to pick up the fragments. The back of her hand was scratched by porcelain stubble, and blood seeped out. Before she could stand up, Zhao Tiezhu's belt had already been pulled off and he hit her twice on the back.

"Who gave you leftover food? Huh? Is it easy for me to make money?"

Sun Guiying lay on the ground, not daring to cry. There were footsteps outside the courtyard gate that stopped for a moment and then slowly walked away - it was the neighbor Lao Zhou. He probably came out to urinate. Hearing the noise, he stopped and went back again. The whole street heard it, but seemed to hear nothing.

That night she did not get on the kang, but knelt on the cold tile floor in the kitchen while Zhao Tiezhu fell asleep by himself. At dawn, her eyes were swollen and she made breakfast. Zhao Tiezhu finished eating half of the steamed buns and went out to work like a normal person. Before leaving, he told her: "Find out the cotton-padded jacket at noon, it's getting cold."

Sun Guiying stood at the entrance of the courtyard, watching his back disappear at the end of the street, feeling confused. She didn't know if this was considered "living a good life", but she only knew that from that night on, she learned to smell the alcohol on Zhao Tiezhu's body before he came in, learned to fill the rice bowl, put the shoes in order, and keep her mouth shut.

Experienced wives on the street will teach newcomers: "Don't talk back. If you talk back, you'll get ten more swats. When you kneel, keep your back straight. Don't lie there like a dead fish. Men will be more angry." It was Mrs. Liu who said this. The man named Liu Mandun, Zhao Tiezhu's senior sister, was a blacksmith, and his fists were harder than iron pillars. There is an old scar on Mrs. Liu's right cheekbone. She doesn't cover it when she wears short-sleeved shirts in summer. She says she "dropped it herself." People on the street know about it and no one will point it out.

On a Saturday at the end of October, the factory got off work early. The men moved the folding tables to the middle of the street and played five-pound chess - not playing chess, but gambling. Whoever loses goes home to "take it out on his wife." This is the unwritten rule of Hongqi Street. It has been said for half a life that it is tougher than the labor discipline in the factory.

Zhao Tiezhu was unlucky that day, losing three games in a row, and his face was as dark as the bottom of a pot. Liu Mandun patted him on the shoulder and smiled: "Tie Zhu, go home and practice. Don't let people say that you are weak at the card table and you are weak at home."

The crowd booed. Sun Guiying was returning from carrying water from the public well. The pole creaked on her shoulders. When she heard laughter, her face turned pale. She quickened her pace and walked home, spilling water all the way.

When Zhao Tiezhu came in, she had already filled the water tank and was squatting on the ground to wipe the wet area just now. She didn't even look up, knowing she wouldn't be able to escape tonight.

"Come here."

Sun Guiying put down the rag and walked up to him, lowering her head. Zhao Tiezhu sat on the edge of the kang, untied his belt, folded it in half, and patted it in his palms, making a dull leather sound that was recognized by men on the street.

"Did those people on the street just look at the way you were walking carrying water?"

"No...no..."

"Lie." Zhao Tiezhu grabbed her hair and pulled her under the kang, "Liu Mandun said, you twisted your waist like in the drama, which is eye-catching. I am losing money, and you are showing off outside. You make me unable to hold my head up in front of my brothers?"

Before Sun Guiying could defend herself, the first leather belt had already fallen down and hit her thigh, causing a burning ridge to appear. She bit her lip, tasting blood. The second and third blows were on the back and buttocks. Zhao Tiezhu hit him more and more fiercely, cursing like "bitch", "shameless", and "shameless". She would memorize these words later and become numb, but that night every sentence was like a nail driven into her flesh.

The courtyard door was not closed tightly, and someone spoke outside. The voice was not loud, but clear: "The Tiezhu family has started again."

"It's normal, I'm just a loser."

"His wife is new, has she been beaten?"

"You will know after a few beatings whether it has been beaten or not."

When Sun Guiying heard these conversations, shame came up first than pain. She wanted to shout, to close the door, to run away - but she knew she couldn't. On this street, the cry of a woman in no house is a secret; and the sound of a man's belt in no house is treated as an "accident" that requires intervention.

After the beating, half of Zhao Tiezhu's anger was vented, and the rest turned into something else. He pushed her on the edge of the bed, pulled open her pants, and entered her from behind despite her refusal and pleas. Sun Guiying buried her face into the pillow and suppressed her sobs into breathy sounds. This was not sex, she knew it - this was a continuation of punishment, the face he had lost in front of his brothers, and he wanted to get it back twice as much from her.

After he was done, Zhao Tiezhu lit a cigarette, blew out smoke rings, and said in a calm tone, as if he had just repaired a screw: "Go get some hot water, I'll wash it up. Liu Mandun and the others will come tomorrow, you can cook two more dishes, don't lose my people."

Sun Guiying stood up on the edge of the Kang, her legs still shaking. She responded, walked into the kitchen, turned her back to the mirror, and saw the red marks on her back, like a piece of skin covered with writing. She suddenly remembered that before she got married, her mother held her hand and said, "When you get married, you have to be sensible."

What does it mean to be sensible? She used to think that she was diligent, obedient, and would have sons. Now she understands: being sensible in Hongqi Street also includes not running around when being beaten, not shouting when being called to bed, washing the man's socks white the next day, and bowing his head in front of the neighbors as if nothing happened last night.

At noon on Sunday, Liu Mandun and Lao Zhou came as expected. Sun Guiying was working in the kitchen wearing an apron. Mrs. Liu came in to help her choose vegetables and said in a low voice: "Don't blame Tie Zhu, this street is like this. If you are good, he will beat you lightly. If you are not good, it will be the same for any man."

Sun Guiying's hand stopped in mid-air, wanting to say something, but in the end she just nodded.

The men drank at the dinner table, but the women did not serve. They served food back and forth between the kitchen and the back room. Liu Mantun drank too much, pointed at Sun Guiying and said to Zhao Tiezhu: "Tie Zhu, I think those marks on your wife's butt are not gone yet. Don't be too heavy next time. If you break them, who will cook for you?"

Everyone laughed again. Zhao Tiezhu also laughed and served Liu Mancunman a glass of wine: "Brother Mancunman, do you feel sorry for me or your sister-in-law?"

"I feel sorry for you. As a wife, you have to know how to use it."

Sun Guiying was standing behind the door curtain holding a plate of fried cabbage. Her stomach churns when she heard these words. She remembered that when Zhao Tiezhu pressed down on her last night, in addition to pain and fear, she also had an indescribable tremor that was suppressed by shame. She hated this tremor and hated herself for actually letting out a sigh of relief at a certain moment because the man finally stopped, and even took the initiative to boil water as if he had completed the task.

That wasn't consent, she told herself repeatedly. That's not it.

But Hongqi Street does not discuss "consent". Here we only discuss whose men are smarter, whose daughters-in-law are more obedient, whose cries spread further on the autumn night, and spread to the whole street about another story of men drinking at the card table.

After the men dispersed that afternoon, Sun Guiying was washing clothes by the well. Shui Bing, with red hands, rubbed Zhao Tiezhu's work clothes hard, as if she wanted to rub something off. Sister-in-law Liu passed by and stood for a while, then sighed: "Guiying, sister, let me tell you the truth. If you want to live, don't even think about running away. A monk can't run away from the temple if you can. On your father's side, with just one word from Tie Zhu, the matchmaker can ruin your reputation in the village."

Sun Guiying didn't raise her head and just asked: "Then... how can I get beaten less?"

Mrs. Liu was silent for a moment and said: "Let him have face. He can hold his head up outside and is too lazy to touch the guy when he comes home. And -" her voice was lower, "sometimes, after he beats you, if you...don't keep crying, don't be like a dead fish. Some men want that after the beating. Just be gentle and he will lose his anger quickly."

Sun Guiying's hand stopped in the water.

This is not to teach her how to love, but to teach her how to survive the winter on a street where beating wives is entertainment. The winter in the north is long, long enough to bend a person's spine and freeze the word "unwilling" under the tongue, never to be uttered again.

At night, Zhao Tiezhu drank some more. He was not drunk, but he talked less. He was lying on the kang, and Sun Guiying blew out the lamp and spread the mattress under her feet. Since she was beaten for the first time, she had not been on the kang again unless she was called up. Zhao Tiezhu suddenly said: "Gui Ying."

She stiffened: "Yeah."

"There will be an inspection in the factory tomorrow. I'll leave early. You can iron that new jacket."

"Got it."

There was silence on the kang for a while. Zhao Tiezhu added: "Last night... I struck too hard. But don't blame me. There are rules on the street. If I lose money, if I don't fight when I go home, my brothers will laugh at me forever."

Sun Guiying opened her eyes in the darkness and did not respond.

Zhao Tiezhu turned over and seemed to be talking to himself: "You are better than the last one. The last one ran back to his parents' house after being beaten twice, which made it ugly. It's good that you didn't run away."

Pretty good.

Sun Guiying buried her face in the pillow, and finally let a little sound leak out. It was very light, like the wind blowing through the leaves of a locust tree. She didn't know if it was crying or something else. Hongqi Street outside the window became quiet, with only the whistle of the train in the distance, one long and one short, like stamping the night of this industrial city.

In this street, beating your wife is not a crime - people here don't say that - but a tacit entertainment between men. It is the price of losing cards, a tonic for face, and a more authentic pastime in winter than storytelling. What about women? Women are the props, the venue, and the person who gets up to make breakfast afterwards.

Sun Guiying slowly stopped shaking on the pillow. She thought of the scar on Mrs. Liu's cheekbones, the ribs on her back that had not yet healed, and the tone in Zhao Tiezhu's voice when he said, "It's good that you didn't run away," and she actually felt a little satisfied.

She didn't know if she would become like Mrs. Liu: teaching the new daughter-in-law at the well how to kneel, how to endure, and how to pick up a man's face after being beaten, like picking up scattered chess pieces.

She only knew that tomorrow she would have to iron her coat, carry water, and walk down the entire Hongqi Street in the eyes of her neighbors—and the next Saturday, the men would move the table to the middle of the street, play five-pound chess, some would lose, and some would go home, with their belts folded in half and clapping in their palms.

The whole street knew that voice.

It's like recognizing the night that sounds again in the autumn night and belongs here.

The old Zhou family lived next door to Zhao Tiezhu. The courtyard wall was short, only reaching the waist. The first time Sun Guiying heard Zhou Caixia cry was on the fifth day after marrying into the street - two days before Zhao Tiezhu moved the belt for the third time. At that time, she was still dazed under the new quilt, and suddenly there was a crisp slap from outside, one, two, seven or eight times in a row, with a short grunt from a woman in the middle, as if someone was grabbing her throat.

Zhao Tiezhu turned over on the kang and muttered: "Old Zhou is playing again."

Sun Guiying opened her eyes on the bunk under her feet and asked: "Play...what?"

"Hey, you don't understand." Zhao Tiezhu smiled vaguely, "Every family on this street plays differently. Tiezhu's family only fights when they lose money. Lao Zhou's family doesn't have to lose, he likes to fight."

Sun Guiying didn't ask again. The slaps stopped, replaced by Lao Zhou's harsh curses, and then there was the sound of the iron door latch, and the street returned to dead silence. She clenched her hands into the corners of the quilt, her nails turning white, but she didn't know what she should be afraid of - afraid of the next door, or afraid that one day Zhao Tiezhu would comment on her lightly like "Old Zhou is having fun again."

Lao Zhou's full name is Zhou Jianguo. He is forty-five years old. He is the deputy director of the machine repair workshop of the factory and is in charge of a tool room. He is thin, with high cheekbones but bright eyes. When he smiles, he reveals a row of smoke-yellow teeth. Zhou Caixia is ten years younger than him and is a veteran of this street. Her father used to be a janitor in the factory. She has been married to Lao Zhou for eight years and has not given birth to a son, only a girl, who is six years old this year and is called Yaya.

The women on Hongqi Street said privately that Zhou Caixia was the "most beaten" person on this street.

It was not because she could bear the beating, but because Lao Zhou beat her for many reasons - the food was salty, the food was bland, Yaya cried and quarreled, her clothes were not hung up, she said too much to a wife, and even if things were not going well in the factory, she had to "do some activities" when she got home. Exercise is a slang term on the street, which means to relax your wife's muscles and muscles, and also to relax your own muscles.

Lao Zhou also has another way of playing, which Zhao Tiezhu and the others call "setting up a banquet."

It wasn't really a banquet, but every Tuesday night, Lao Zhou set up a folding table in the courtyard, called Liu Mandun and Zhao Tiezhu, and brought along two single young workers who liked to join in the fun. They bought two bottles of loose powder and a plate of peanuts, and sat under the courtyard wall to drink. Halfway through the drink, Lao Zhou called Zhou Caixia out and lectured her in public. After training, if anyone feels that it is not interesting enough, Lao Zhou will do it; if someone gets interested, he will also come up with an idea: "Hit the legs, don't slap the face, we have to go to work tomorrow."

The first time Sun Guiying was dragged into such a scene was in mid-November.

Zhao Tiezhu came back from work that day and threw an enamel basin into her arms: "Go to Lao Zhou's house to borrow some soy sauce. Quick."

Sun Guiying took the basin and went out. It was already dark. The door of Lao Zhou's courtyard was open, the lights were bright inside, and the man's laughter was like boiling water. She stood outside the threshold, neither advancing nor retreating. Liu Mandun saw her first and whistled: "Is the little wife of Tiezhu's family here? Come in, come in, don't be restrained."

There were five or six men at one table in the courtyard. Zhou Caixia was kneeling beside the table, not on the ground, but on a bench - her face turned inward, her butt stuck out, her cotton pants lowered to the knees, revealing her two buttocks that had been beaten purple. She put her hands behind her back and tied them with a clothesline. The knot was tied by Lao Zhou, and the knot was so tight that her wrists turned blue.

The basin in Sun Guiying's hand almost fell.

Lao Zhou held the wine bowl without turning his head: "What can I borrow?"

"Soy sauce... soy sauce..."

"Get it from the kitchen yourself." Old Zhou pouted and said to Liu Mandun, "Continue, where did you say that? Yes, Zhou Caixia went to the grain store last week and talked to the young man selling grain stamps. Why are you laughing? I raised you to ogle with wild men?"

Zhou Caixia's shoulders were shaking, and her voice came out from her arms: "I don't have any... I'm just asking if the food stamps can be exchanged for tofu..."

"Still talking back?"

Lao Yiyi kicked the leg of the bench, and Zhou Caixia fell forward, hitting her forehead on the edge of the table with a thud. The men coaxed. Zhao Tiezhu and her husband were also there, sitting and smoking, and looked at Sun Guiying. There was no pain in her eyes, only a warning: Don't be troublesome, don't be embarrassed.

Sun Guiying got into the kitchen as if she was running away. The soy sauce bottle was on the stove, and her hands were shaking so much that she couldn't unscrew the lid. Lao Zhou's voice came from the back room: "Caixia, tell your brothers, where did you go wrong?"

"I...I shouldn't...talk to outsiders..."

"Louder. Let the newcomers to the Tiezhu family also listen to the rules of this street."

Zhou Caixia raised her voice, and every word seemed to be torn out: "I shouldn't laugh with the people in the grain store... I shouldn't embarrass Lao Zhou... I deserve a beating..."

"What should I do if I need a spanking?"

"...please Lao Zhou fight...please brothers watch..."

Sun Guiying's soy sauce finally flowed into the basin, black and bright, fishy and sweet. She should not have listened, but she kept her ears open. Outside, Lao Zhou asked, "Mancun, what kind of guy should I use for a woman like this?"

Liu Mandun was drunk: "The belt is too cheap for her. Use a bamboo grate, the kind used for packaging in the factory. It makes a loud sound but doesn't leave a dead mark - how can you go to work tomorrow if you leave a mark?"

Sun Guiying looked out from the kitchen window and saw Lao Zhouzhen carrying a bundle of thin bamboo slices from the tool room. He tried it in his hand and it was blown by the wind. Zhou Caixia was still kneeling, not begging for mercy, but burying her face deeper, as if she knew she couldn't escape this disaster, and just begged for it to pass quickly.

When the first bamboo grate went down, the sound was sharper and dryer than the belt. With a snap, a red edge immediately appeared on the buttocks. Zhou Caixia's back arched like a shrimp and she squeezed out a sound from her nose. The second and third blows, Lao Zhou explained while hitting, like demonstrating how to move a turning tool in a workshop: "Did you see it? It needs to be like this, do it with your wrists, don't use force. Beating your wife is like repairing a machine, you need to know how to do it."

The men nodded, commented, and gestured. A young worker asked: "Director Zhou, what should I do if my wife is beaten and runs away?"

Lao Zhou smiled: "Run? Where are you running? The registered permanent residence is in the factory, and the food stamps are in my pocket. Her father is still guarding the gate. After running away, I will lead people to catch her and beat her again in front of you. That is not entertainment, it is setting rules."

Everyone laughed again. Sun Guiying was standing under the window holding a basin, her legs were as weak as if she had just finished carrying two loads of water.

After the tenth blow, Zhou Caixia's voice changed. It was no longer just about pain, but mixed with a kind of broken breathing. Lao Zhou stopped, threw the bamboo grate on the table, and said to Zhao Tiezhu: "Tie Zhu, your wife just came here and doesn't understand. You can teach her when you get back. Women can't get used to it. If they get used to it, they will sit on your head and shit."

Zhao Tiezhu smiled apologetically: "Yes, Brother Zhou. Why don't I... teach you slowly?"

Lao Zhou waved his hand as if sending a formal message: "Okay, Caixia, thank you brothers for supervising me. Go and scoop the soy sauce for Tiezhu's wife - don't shake your hands. If you shake, you will be punished."

Zhou Caixia got down from the bench, limping into the kitchen without fully lifting her cotton pants. For the first time, Sun Guiying took a close look at the "most beaten" woman on the street: her face was pale, her temples were wet, but her eye sockets were dry and there were no tears. She took the basin from Sun Guiying's hand and filled it for her. Her movements were steady, as if she was not the one who was tied to the bamboo grate in the courtyard just now.

"First time meeting?" Zhou Caixia asked, her voice hoarse.

Sun Guiying nodded.

"Just get used to it." Zhou Caixia handed the basin back to her, twitching the corner of her mouth, not really smiling, "Don't stand in a daze at the door of the courtyard. They don't like women to daze, as if they are watching a joke. Just take it and leave."

Sun Guiying walked out and when she passed the center of the courtyard, the men were still drinking. Lao Zhou raised the bowl to her: "Ask Tiezhu for me. Tell him that if he loses money again on Saturday, he will come to me and I will lend him a bamboo grate - more respectable than a belt."

Everyone laughed. Sun Guiying lowered her head and walked out of the courtyard quickly, the light behind her burning the back of her neck, as if those eyes were still attached to her.

After returning home, Zhao Tiezhu took the soy sauce and smelled it on his nose: "Just borrow some soy sauce, why have you been away for so long?"

Sun Guiying said nothing. Zhao Tiezhu glanced at her, took her silence as obedient, and didn't ask any more questions.

At night, while Sun Guiying was lying under her feet, there was another sound from the next door - not a beating, but a low-pitched conversation between Lao Zhou and Zhou Caixia, intermittently, like wind leaking through the cracks in the wall.

"...Today...Have Yaya slept?"

"Sleep. I'll coax you for a long time."

"She didn't wake up when she hit you?"

"No."

Silence. Then Lao Zhou said: "Next time when setting up a banquet, tie the knot loosely. Today Liu Mandun said it was too tight and it doesn't look good."

Zhou Caixia said "hmm", but she couldn't tell whether it was a promise or a habit.

Sun Guiying covered her head with a quilt. She suddenly understood what Zhao Tiezhu meant when he said "each family plays the game differently" - Zhao Tiezhu beat her with the shame and anger of losing money, and the urgency of trying to make up for it in front of his brothers; Lao Zhou beat Zhou Caixia, but it was like a fixed program, with an audience, lines, props, and even "decency". One is because I lost, and the other is because I like it.

She couldn't choose either way.

Sure enough, Saturday arrived again. Zhao Tiezhu's luck was average this time, and he lost a small amount. When he returned home, his face was not too bad. He only pressed Sun Guiying on the edge of the kang and slapped her several times without touching the belt. Sun Guiying breathed a sigh of relief, thinking that this night would be lighter.

In the middle of the night, she was awakened by the noise in the middle of the street - folding tables, dice and swearing. The loser this time was Ma San, a welder from another family two doors down. Ma San was drunk and dragged his wife all the way back to the courtyard from the middle of the street, and the whole street followed. Sun Guiying counted the slaps on the other side in the darkness, one, two, and then Zhao Tiezhu turned over and muttered: "Ma San is a bitch, his hands are really dark."

"...Don't you go and take a look?" Sun Guiying herself didn't know why she asked this question.

Zhao Tiezhu was stunned, as if he heard some joke: "What are you looking at? Watching my wife get beaten? I have nothing to do?" He stretched out his hand and patted her on the head, not hard, but with a sleepy boredom, "Sleep with you. There are a lot of lively things on this street at night. You will get used to it gradually. Old Zhou's house is on Tuesday, Masan's house is on Saturday, my house - it depends on my luck. Do you count, how many days is this street quiet?"

Sun Guiying calculated in the dark. Can't figure it out. She only heard Ma San's wife's cry, which was muffled by the door, and further away, someone's radio was playing Shan Tianfang's storytelling. The storyteller's loud voice could not cover up this series of fragmentary night sounds that belonged to Hongqi Street.

At dawn, she went to the well to fetch water. Zhou Caixia was already there, the bucket was full of water, and she was applying ice to her face. There is a new fingerprint on the right side of the face, from the cheekbone to the corner of the mouth, which is purple and green.

The two looked at each other. Sun Guiying wanted to say something, but Zhou Caixia spoke first. Her voice was as calm as saying that the price of cabbage had increased today: "Old Zhou was happy last night because Ma San lost money, so he drank two more drinks. He went home to practice with me and said that Ma San's playing was too rough, which was outrageous - I had to show Ma San's daughter-in-law how to play in an organized manner."

Sun Guiying's barrel rope was tied around her shoulders, causing severe pain.

Zhou Caixia fetched the water, picked it up, and walked past Sun Guiying. She paused and only left one sentence: "You can bear it better than I thought. Tolerance is good. When you can't help it - don't help it in front of them. Go back to the house and cry again."

She's gone. Sun Guiying stood by the well and watched her figure turn into the gate of Lao Zhou's courtyard. The courtyard door is closed today, unlike the night when the banquet was set out. But Sun Guiying knew that it was the same inside and outside - this family, the family before her, and the family after her, all used wife beating as a pastime, a program, and a tacit entertainment between men on their own nights.

And she was carrying the empty bucket home. When passing by the wall of Lao Zhou's courtyard, she heard Yaya reciting multiplication tables in the back room, her milky milky sounds, one by one makes one, and two by two makes four. The sound was clean, like the only part of the street that had not been beaten with belts and bamboo grates.

Sun Guiying stopped for a step at the door of the courtyard, took a deep breath, and pushed the door open. Zhao Tiezhu is still sleeping. She lit a fire, washed rice, and pressed last night's slaps, the sound of bamboo grates next door, and the Masan family's cries into the bottom of the pot, like layers of ashes in the long northern winter.

Tuesday will come again. Will come again on Saturday. The table at Lao Zhou's house will still be set, Zhao Tiezhu's luck will change, Ma San will still drag his wife across the whole street - and the night on Hongqi Street will ring again and again, like clocks, like habits, like the most important and least worth mentioning entertainment for men on this street.

The third house to the west of the locust tree at the street corner has a courtyard door painted black, which is a bit taller than the other houses. Hu Kui lives here.

People on Hongqi Street don't call him Street Fighter - that's a word from the movie, it sounds fake. They are called "Kui Ye", or simply "Hu Tou". Hu Kui is forty-two years old, a retired soldier, and serves as the deputy section chief of the factory security department, in charge of access control and night patrol. He is big, with broad shoulders, and walks with a wind. He wears an old military medal on his chest. No one dares to ask what year it is from. He also has a family and a wife, just like other men.

Sun Guiying heard what Zhao Tiezhu said: "In this street, beating your wife is a pastime. Being beaten by a beard is a deadly pastime."

When Zhao Tiezhu said this, there was a strange respect and fear in his tone, like talking about the high-speed steel knife in the machine tool - it is not used normally, but it will cause blood when used.

Hu Tou doesn't like to set up a banquet like Lao Zhou, and he doesn't often play Wujin chess. His entertainment was even rarer: whoever he deemed "unworthy of being punished" would be dragged under the locust tree and beaten in front of everyone in the house - pulling hair, kneeling on bricks, belts, bamboo grates, admitting mistakes in public, and having to say "I deserve a beating, please beat me". The attack was as harsh as the one who beat up the wife on this street, but it fell on the man.

At the beginning of December, the factory gave out another round of welfare: two kilograms of pig head meat and a bottle of Sanbai per person. The men start drinking at noon, and there is always a table under the locust tree in the middle of the street. None of the women beside the well dared to move or help them—not because they didn't want to, but because they didn't dare. Mrs. Hu swept around, her eyes like whips, falling on Zhou Caixia's face, then on Mrs. Liu's face, and finally on Sun Guiying's face.

"Did you see that one from Tiezhu's family?"

Sun Guiying nodded, her throat tight. The sour smell just now wafted over a few feet away, and her stomach turned. She resisted vomiting, fearing that vomiting would be considered "disgraceful to Mrs. Hu".

Mrs. Hu came closer and pinched her chin as if weighing an object: "Keep your mouth clean. You'd better not step on your man's line and my man's line. If you step on it, Xiao Lu is like this, but it's still light." She let go of her hand and added, "If you can't stand being beaten, Tiezhu will teach you. If you can't deal with it, I'll teach you. Understand?"

Sun Guiying whispered: "...understand."

Mrs. Hu nodded with satisfaction, picked up the empty bucket she had brought late, and fetched water. Her movements were normal, as if she had just slipped on the edge of the well. The sound of water rushed past the nosebleeds on the edge of the well platform, past the mud prints, and past the dead silence just now. Then everyone slowly dispersed, and the bucket rope sounded again, but no one said anything anymore - you have to choose the time, the person to speak, and the presence or absence of Mrs. Hu.

Sister-in-law Liu walked past Sun Guiying, paused for half a second, moved her lips, and finally said nothing. She just carried the bucket away, her shoulders and back a little more bent than usual. Zhou Caixia hugged Yaya with her eyes downward, as if she was afraid that raising her eyes even an inch would cause a foul. Aunt Wang put the vegetable basket in her arms and changed hands, muttering "It's getting cold", as if she was looking for a safe word out of thin air. Sun Guiying was the last one to fill up the water. The well rope slipped in her hand, causing pain in her palm, as if to remind her: Don't forget. The water in the bucket swayed, causing her shoulder bones to ache. When passing by the gate of Hu Touhei's courtyard, it was quiet inside, as if nothing had happened; when passing by Masan's house, she heard the child crying inside the door, and she walked faster. Passing by the gate of Xiaolu's courtyard, a man could be heard scolding her: "You're such a loser, how did you become so virtuous..." She didn't dare to stop, and just tightened the barrel rope again.

Entering the hospital, Zhao Tiezhu was wiping the oil hand brought back from the machine tool. He raised his head and asked, "Why are you so slow?"

"...the well...crowded."

"Why are you squeezing? Just fetch water." Zhao Tiezhu glanced at her and didn't ask any questions. After wiping his hands, he pinched her face, not too hard. "Don't talk at night. The factory has been having a rough couple of days, so don't mess with me."

Sun Guiying said "hmm" and went into the kitchen to pour water. His hands were still shaking, and water spilled over half of the basin. She didn't tell anything about Xiao Lu's wife - why did she tell her? Let Zhao Tiezhu also comment on "Sister Hu's hands are dirty"? Or should Zhao Tiezhu say, "Don't go to the well"? There is only one well in this street, and if you have to cook food and drink water, you won’t miss a beat if you tell me.

When the meal was on the table, Zhao Tiezhu took two bites and suddenly asked: "Is Mrs. Hu at the well?"

Sun Guiying paused with her chopsticks: "...here."

"Just remember it now." Zhao Tiezhu nodded his chopsticks on the rim of the bowl, like ringing a bell, "Hu Tou Hu, Sister-in-law Hu, don't step on the two lines between men and women. If you step on it, I will beat you first, then Hu Tou will beat me, and Hu Tou will beat you again - three times, it's not worth it."

Sun Guiying lowered her head to eat, with rice stuck in her throat. Zhao Tiezhu added: "That daughter-in-law of Xiaolu's family still doesn't know how to shut up after half a year. It will happen sooner or later. Don't imitate her."

"...I don't want to learn."

"Yes." Zhao Tiezhu seemed to be relieved, but also seemed to be just casually saying, "As long as you respect Mrs. Hu. Respect is not to be afraid, but not to give her an excuse."

Sun Guiying didn't understand why she wasn't given the excuse. She only knew that the sour smell from the well just now was still in her nose. When she was washing the dishes, she opened the window to let some air in, and the cold wind came in, but it couldn't wash away the smell, as if it had already been written into her memory.

She filled the water tank, and in front of her eyes was always Xiao Lu's wife's swollen and deformed face stuck behind other people's buttocks, Mrs. Hu's cold voice counting "one, two, three", and Zhou Caixia's hand covering Yaya's eyes. She suddenly understood: Sister Hu among women and Hu Tou among men are the same kind of person - they cannot be offended, cannot be avoided, and they have to learn to retract their eyes even when they look at her; and these women beside the well, including herself, are the audience in this play, and they are also characters who may be picked up at any time next time.

It was getting dark, and Zhou Caixia walked past the courtyard wall with Yaya in her arms, her steps hurried, as if she was afraid of the dark. Sun Guiying was collecting clothes on the rope, and Zhou Caixia paused for half a second, her lips turned white, and finally she just said: "...Don't learn from Aunt Liu. Don't learn from Aunt Wang. And don't learn from me. Just do what Aunt Hu tells you to do - you can survive on this street."

Yaya asked: "Mom, why did Aunt Hu hit Aunt Lu?"

Zhou Caixia covered the child's mouth and dragged her away. Sun Guiying stood under the rope with no clothes left. Her hands were cold and her heart was even colder.

At night, sporadic sounds resounded on Hongqi Street: someone's man's curse, someone's daughter-in-law's low hum. Sun Guiying was sitting on the bunk under her feet when she heard the door of Hutou Courtyard slamming shut in the distance - Mrs. Hu had probably returned as well. She didn't know if Mrs. Hu would be as straight as she had been during the day when she came in, and whether she would kneel down in front of Hu Tou; she only knew that she would have to go to the well to fetch water tomorrow, and Mrs. Hu's words of "see you at the well tomorrow" were like a nail nailed to Xiao Lu's wife's body and her heart.

She closed her eyes and tucked the corners of the quilt tightly, as if tucking in a rule she hadn't yet understood. The wind rustled through the locust trees outside, and it sounded like a woman was whispering beside the well, or it was just the wind - she couldn't tell, and she didn't dare to tell.
